Design Philosophy

I believe good design starts with understanding people. Before thinking about how something should look, I want to understand what someone needs, what they're struggling with, and how I can make their experience better. The goal isn't to add more to a screen, but to make it easier for people to get where they need to go.

I'm drawn to projects that take something complicated and make it easier to understand. Whether it's medication information or a digital service, I pay attention to how information is organized, how people move through an experience, and what they need to know at each step. I want my designs to be clear without losing the details that matter.

I also believe a design needs to work beyond the ideal scenario. Real content doesn't always fit neatly into a layout, and people don't always use things the way we expect. I like experimenting with real data, testing ideas, and building in code because it helps me understand what works in practice, not just in a mockup.

Outside of usability and problem-solving, I care about how an experience feels. My interest in art, festivals, and live events has shaped my appreciation for the small details that make people feel welcome and connected. I want to bring that same thoughtfulness into my work, creating experiences that are useful, approachable, and made with people in mind.
